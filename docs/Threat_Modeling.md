# Threat Modeling

Life Mission should model future cyber risk at multiple layers.

## System Layers

- Hardware and firmware
- Operating systems
- Applications
- Identity and access
- Networks and communications
- Cloud and infrastructure
- Supply chains and dependencies
- Human and organizational processes
- AI-enabled systems

## Threat Model Components

For each research scenario, document:

- Assets
- Trust boundaries
- Preconditions
- Capabilities required
- Likely attacker incentives
- Relevant historical analogues
- Defensive controls
- Expected indicators
- Uncertainties
- Potential consequences

## Evolutionary View

Threat modeling should not assume attackers simply select from a fixed catalog of techniques. The research should consider how new combinations become possible when technology or defenses change.

## Forecasting Use

Threat models provide the context needed to turn a generic signal into a meaningful forecast. A pattern such as increased automation, for example, should be examined in relation to the systems that automation can affect, the incentives for using it, and the defenses likely to constrain it.

## Safety Boundary

Threat models may describe hypothetical adversarial behavior for defensive analysis, but operational experimentation belongs only in authorized and isolated environments.
