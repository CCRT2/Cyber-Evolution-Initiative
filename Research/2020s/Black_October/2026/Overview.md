# Black October 2026: Research Overview

## Purpose

Black October is a Cyber Evolution Initiative (CEI) research series that collects and examines significant cybersecurity incidents publicly reported during October. The 2026 edition is a starting point for building a documented, source-backed record.

The name is a research label, not a statistical conclusion. This project does **not** assume that October has more cyberattacks than other months. Establishing a seasonal pattern would require a consistent dataset covering multiple years and months.

## Research questions

1. What significant cybersecurity incidents were publicly reported during October 2026?
2. Which sectors, regions, and types of organizations were affected?
3. What incident categories appear in the collected reports (for example, ransomware, exploitation of vulnerabilities, credential compromise, or data exposure)?
4. What is known about the initial access, affected systems, and operational impact?
5. Which details are confirmed by primary sources, and which remain allegations or unverified claims?
6. What patterns, if any, are supported by the evidence?

## Inclusion criteria

Include an incident when:
- A credible source reports that a cybersecurity incident occurred or was disclosed during October 2026; and
- The report provides enough detail to identify the affected organization or service and the general nature of the incident.

Record the incident's occurrence date separately from its discovery, disclosure, and publication dates. An incident reported in October may have begun earlier.

Do not count ordinary service outages as cyberattacks unless evidence supports malicious activity. Place non-malicious infrastructure failures in the [Infrastructure Incidents](../../Infrastructure_Incidents/README.md) collection and cross-reference them only when useful.

## Evidence and confidence

Prioritize primary sources such as affected organizations, government advisories, law-enforcement statements, and incident-response reports. Use reputable journalism and specialist research to add context or corroboration.

For each important claim:
- Link the source and record its publication date.
- Separate confirmed facts from claims attributed to a source.
- Note conflicting accounts and missing information.
- Avoid treating a threat actor's claim as proof.
- Do not infer successful compromise from an attempted or blocked attack.

Use a confidence label for CEI's assessment:
- **High:** Supported by direct primary evidence or multiple independent, reliable sources.
- **Moderate:** Supported by credible reporting, but important details remain unconfirmed.
- **Low:** Based on limited, indirect, or uncorroborated reporting.

Confidence applies to the specific assessment, not automatically to every detail in an incident report.

## Suggested incident categories

- Ransomware and extortion
- Exploitation of vulnerabilities
- Credential theft and account compromise
- Data breach or exposure
- Malware and intrusion activity
- Supply-chain compromise
- Distributed denial of service
- Espionage or state-linked activity (attribute carefully)
- Other cybersecurity incidents

An incident may fit more than one category. Explain the classification rather than forcing it into a single label.

## Analysis limitations

This collection is not a complete census of all cyber incidents. Public reporting is uneven: some incidents are disclosed late, some are never disclosed, and reporting volume can reflect media attention or disclosure practices rather than the true number of attacks.

Do not claim that October is unusually dangerous, the most active month, or part of a rising trend based only on this edition. A seasonal comparison would need:
- The same incident definitions across every month;
- A consistent set of sources and collection method;
- Multiple years of data;
- Deduplication of incidents covered by several outlets; and
- Clear treatment of unknown dates and reporting delays.

## 2026 edition workflow

1. Identify candidate incidents from reliable reporting.
2. Verify the organization, event type, and relevant dates.
3. Create an entry using [the incident template](./Incident_Template.md).
4. Add the entry to the incident register in `README.md`.
5. Review sources, attribution, confidence, and duplicate coverage.
6. Publish a short findings summary only when enough evidence has been collected.

## Research integrity

CEI should document what the evidence supports, what it does not support, and what remains unknown. Corrections should be recorded transparently. The goal is a useful research record, not a dramatic headline count.
