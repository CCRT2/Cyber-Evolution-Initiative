# 🕒 Timelines

This folder is for putting cyber history in order.

A timeline by itself isn't the final research. It's the record we build before we start asking what the record means.

That matters because events that look unrelated when they're viewed one at a time can start to look very different when they're placed next to each other.

## What Goes In Here

Timelines can cover:

- Major cybersecurity incidents
- Malware outbreaks
- Vulnerability disclosures
- Important defensive changes
- AI security events
- Major computing and networking milestones
- Security policy and regulation
- Research breakthroughs that changed the field

## What We Should Capture

When possible, an entry should make clear:

- **When** it happened
- **What** happened
- **Who** was involved or affected
- **What evidence** supports the date or event
- **What changed afterward**
- **What later events may connect to it**

Not every historical event will have a perfect timeline. Public reporting can be incomplete, contradictory, or released long after the event itself.

That's part of the record too.

## Why CEI Cares About Timelines

CEI is interested in evolution, and evolution needs time.

If we want to study whether techniques spread, disappear, combine, or come back in a different form, we need to know when those things happened and what information was available at the time.

```text
Event
  ↓
Timeline
  ↓
Connections
  ↓
Historical Pattern
  ↓
Research Question
```

## One Important Rule

Don't rewrite history with information from the future.

A forecast made in 2026 should be judged using what was known when the forecast was made, not by quietly adding facts that appeared in 2027.

That sounds obvious, but it is one of the easiest ways to fool ourselves when doing historical analysis.

## Current Example

The repository currently contains a timeline for the 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face incident. That timeline can be compared with the larger incident record as CEI grows.

## Building This Over Time

The long-term goal is for these timelines to become connected instead of isolated pages.

A vulnerability should be able to connect to incidents that used it. An incident should be able to connect to the techniques involved. A technique should connect to earlier and later examples.

That's when the timeline stops being just a list of dates and starts becoming useful research data.
